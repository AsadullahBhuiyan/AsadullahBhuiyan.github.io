Research

Learning from almost nothing

{% include project-citation.html project="learning" %}

We study neural networks trained on heavily corrupted inputs with clean labels. Our numerical experiments show that multilayer perceptrons can predict well above chance even when individual training images are almost entirely noise.

In the high-noise, infinite-width limit, we derive an interpretable leading-order decision rule governed by overlaps with centered class means. Within this limit, the rule is universal across a broad class of MLP architectures. We use controlled numerical experiments to test how well this effective description captures the behavior of finite-width networks.

{% include paper-figure.html id="learning_robustness" %}

My contributions

  • Initiated the project and recruited three collaborators.
  • Derived the universal leading-order decision rule using symmetry and linear response within neural tangent kernel theory.
  • Used Python and PyTorch to study ensembles of finite-width networks across levels of input corruption and test the theoretical predictions.
{% include paper-figure.html id="learning_theory" %}

The analytical result applies to the high-noise kernel regime, and the numerical comparison uses fitted global coefficients. Outside this limit, trained networks need not follow the same decision rule.

Topological quantum dynamics

{% include project-citation.html project="quantum" %}

We use symmetry and topology to classify monitored free-fermion dynamics and design adaptive quantum circuits that prepare topological states.

Our work establishes a dynamical bulk–boundary correspondence connecting the topology of the dynamics to that of their steady states. We also determine which symmetry classes admit symmetry-preserving Gaussian measurements without post-selection and use these results to construct adaptive state-preparation protocols.

{% include paper-figure.html id="quantum_classification" %}

My contributions

  • Developed Lie-algebraic proofs underlying the topological classification and established conditions for the existence of Gaussian measurements in the relevant classes.
  • Designed and analyzed a measurement-feedback protocol for preparing disordered ensembles of free-fermion topological states.
  • Derived an effective description of the protocol’s dynamics and used covariance methods to determine its relaxation rate.
  • Numerically studied the protocol’s topological phase transitions using Python and PyTorch.
{% include paper-figure.html id="quantum_protocol" %}

The ideal construction uses exponentially localized operations, while the numerical protocol uses finite-range operations. We also distinguish observables evaluated along individual trajectories from those constructed after averaging over trajectories.

Chiral critical state ensembles in adaptive quantum dynamics

{% include project-citation.html project="ongoing" %}

In ongoing follow-up work, we investigate the critical and chiral properties of topological domain-wall modes prepared by measurement and feedback. I compare entanglement, mutual information, and charge fluctuations across system sizes to characterize these boundary-state ensembles.

Further doctoral research

Monitored bosonic dynamics

I studied how random unitary evolution interspersed with measurements affects entanglement dynamics in Gaussian bosonic circuits.

  • Proved an exponential upper bound on the mean second Rényi entropy in an all-to-all Gaussian bosonic circuit with Haar-random passive unitary gates and single-mode heterodyne measurements.
  • Constructed a replica spin-chain theory describing entropy dynamics in a 1+1D monitored, stochastic Gaussian bosonic circuit.
  • Validated analytical results numerically by simulating entanglement dynamics using covariance methods and Python.
