Learning from almost nothing

How neural networks survive heavy input corruption

We study neural networks trained on heavily corrupted inputs with intact labels. Our numerical experiments show that multilayer perceptrons retain predictive information even when individual training images are almost entirely noise.

In the high-noise, infinite-width limit, our analysis identifies a leading predictor governed by overlaps with centered class means. The leading decision rule is universal across a broad class of MLP architectures within this limit. Controlled numerical experiments test how this effective description compares with finite-width networks.

My contributions

  • Initiated the project from numerical observations, recruited three collaborators, and organized our research meetings.
  • Developed and wrote the permutation-symmetry and linear-response explanation of the centroid predictor within neural tangent kernel theory.
  • Guided the complementary high-noise expansion and used Python/PyTorch experiments to compare the theory with ensembles of finite-width networks.
  • Contributed substantially to the manuscript’s writing and organization.

Topological quantum dynamics

Classification and adaptive preparation of topological states

We develop a symmetry and topology classification of monitored free-fermion dynamics and construct adaptive protocols for preparing topological states.

Our work connects many-body evolution operators with single-particle transfer matrices through a dynamical bulk–boundary correspondence. It also determines which symmetry classes allow symmetry-preserving, post-selection-free Gaussian measurements, then uses these results to construct adaptive topological-state preparation protocols.

{% include paper-figure.html id="quantum_classification" %}

My contributions

  • Developed Lie-algebraic classification proofs and existence proofs for symmetry-preserving Gaussian measurements, and wrote the technical appendices.
  • Constructed and analyzed a finite-range measurement-feedback protocol for preparing disordered ensembles of free-fermion topological states.
  • Originated an effective Lindbladian description and used closed covariance equations to determine the relaxation rate of the ideal preparation dynamics.
  • Performed independent numerical studies of the protocol and its phase transitions, created the classification figure above, and contributed substantially to the main text.

The ideal construction with exponentially localized operations and the finite-range numerical protocol are distinct. We also distinguish observables resolved along individual trajectories from those formed after averaging over trajectories.

Chiral critical state ensembles

Chiral critical state ensembles in topological adaptive quantum circuits
Asadullah Bhuiyan, Haining Pan, and Chao-Ming Jian.

Our ongoing work investigates critical and chiral properties of topological domain-wall modes prepared by the measurement-feedback protocol. I compare entanglement, mutual information, and charge-fluctuation diagnostics across system sizes to characterize these boundary-state ensembles. Establishing their chiral structure remains a research objective.

Further doctoral research

Monitored bosonic dynamics

I study how stochastic measurements purify Gaussian bosonic systems and how their entropy dynamics can be described analytically.

  • Proved an exponential upper bound on mean second Rényi entropy for an all-to-all Gaussian circuit with Haar-random passive gates and single-mode heterodyne measurements.
  • Constructed a replica spin-chain description of entropy dynamics in a one-dimensional stochastic bosonic circuit.
  • Investigated covariance dynamics numerically, including stochastic Riccati formulations, and identified truncation artifacts in finite-Hilbert-space simulations.

My interests in this area include dissipation, purification, and effective descriptions of stochastic quantum dynamics.

Earlier research

Driven systems, quantum matter, and biophysics

At the University of Alberta, I combined analytical and numerical methods across several settings:

  • Driven finite lattices: studied edge-localized Schrödinger cat states and the role of boundary conditions in reconciling continuum and tight-binding descriptions.
  • Quantum dots: investigated Landau levels and edge states, separating gauge-invariant densities and currents from numerical eigenvector ambiguities.
  • Singular potentials: coauthored work on bound states of the one-dimensional pseudoharmonic oscillator.
  • Correlated electrons: developed an iterative MATLAB Green’s-function solver for the two-dimensional Hubbard–Holstein model.
  • Microtubule ensembles: contributed software, formal analysis, and validation to a study of electrical properties under cell-like conditions.

At Cornell, I also studied interacting-edge conductance and magnetic-impurity renormalization in fractional topological-insulator models.
