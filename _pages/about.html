About

Asadullah Bhuiyan

Ph.D. Candidate in Theoretical Physics · Cornell University

I’m Asad, a Ph.D. candidate in theoretical physics at Cornell University, advised by Chao-Ming Jian. I study the physics of learning and stochastic quantum dynamics, combining analytical theory with numerical experiments.

I am particularly interested in statistical physics and kernel descriptions of neural networks, AI interpretability, and how data structure, noise, and training shape learning. My quantum research spans open and monitored systems, topology, state preparation, and critical phenomena.

Teaching & mentoring

At Cornell, I am a seminar instructor for AEP 4230: Statistical Physics (Fall 2026), for which I developed original coursework on statistical physics methods for neural networks. I previously taught seminars and laboratories for PHYS 2214: Oscillations, Waves, and Quantum Physics, and seminars for PHYS 1201: Why the Sky is Blue.

I mentored a high-school student through a year of weekly meetings, from introductory quantum mechanics to a Qiskit project on entanglement dynamics in open quantum circuits. The project received third-place awards at the Massachusetts State and Region IV Science and Engineering Fairs in 2025.

I have also advised undergraduates on graduate-school applications and mentored first-year graduate students.

Outreach

With my research group, I co-developed a moiré-pattern activity for middle-school students and presented it at two Cornell-hosted teacher workshops. I also presented physics demonstrations for K–12 students at the Ithaca Sciencenter.

Research support & experience

My doctoral research has been supported by an NSERC Postgraduate Scholarship–Doctoral and a Cornell Graduate Fellowship. Before Cornell, I worked on theoretical and computational projects at the University of Alberta, and on Python test automation during an internship at Microchip Technology.
