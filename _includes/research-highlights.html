Analytic theory of neural-network learning under input corruption

We study neural networks trained on heavily corrupted inputs with clean labels. Our numerical experiments show that multilayer perceptrons retain substantial predictive power even when individual training images are almost entirely noise.

In the high-noise, infinite-width limit, we derive an interpretable leading-order decision rule governed by overlaps with centered class means, universal across a broad class of MLP architectures. Controlled numerical experiments test this effective description in finite-width networks. The comparison uses fitted global coefficients; outside the high-noise kernel regime, networks need not follow the same rule.

Topology and entropy in monitored quantum dynamics

We use symmetry and topology to classify monitored free-fermion dynamics and design adaptive quantum circuits that prepare topological states. A dynamical bulk–boundary correspondence connects the topology of the dynamics to that of their steady states. The ideal preparation scheme uses exponentially localized operations; our numerical implementation uses finite-range operations.

I also studied how measurements and random unitary evolution affect entanglement in Gaussian bosonic circuits. This work combines analytical bounds on entropy in all-to-all circuits, replica spin-chain descriptions of one-dimensional dynamics, and numerical simulations using covariance methods.
