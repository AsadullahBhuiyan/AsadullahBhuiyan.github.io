Hi there! I’m Asad, a PhD candidate in theoretical physics at Cornell working on high-dimensional stochastic systems in quantum many-body physics and machine learning. I combine analytical theory and Python simulations to understand how complex behavior emerges from randomness, interactions, and learning.

I’m especially interested in problems whose underlying structure isn’t obvious: finding the right effective description, building simulations to test it, and turning complex behavior into a clear mechanism. This has drawn me increasingly toward AI alignment, interpretability, and safety—questions I expect to become more urgent in the coming years. These problems remain difficult to formulate precisely, and I believe a physicist’s toolkit can help sharpen the relevant questions and make sense of the complexity involved.

I value clear reasoning, collaboration, intellectual honesty, and empathy, and I’m always open to conversations across research, technology, and decision-making.

Preprint · Under review at PRR

Learning from almost nothing

We study neural networks trained on heavily corrupted inputs. In the high-noise, infinite-width limit, we derive an interpretable decision rule governed by class-centroid overlaps that is universal across a broad class of MLP architectures.

I initiated the project, recruited three collaborators, and developed a symmetry-based linear-response explanation of the centroid rule within neural tangent kernel theory.

Published · Physical Review Research (2026)

Topological classification and adaptive state preparation

We use symmetry and topology to classify monitored free-fermion dynamics and design adaptive quantum circuits that prepare topological states.

I developed classification and existence proofs, derived an effective description of our preparation protocol to determine its relaxation rate, and numerically studied its topological phase transitions.
