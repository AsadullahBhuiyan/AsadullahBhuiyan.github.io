Hi there! I’m Asad.

I’m a PhD candidate in theoretical physics at Cornell, working on high-dimensional stochastic systems in quantum many-body physics and machine learning. I combine analytical theory and Python simulations to understand how complex behavior emerges from randomness, interactions, and learning.

I’m drawn to problems where the right description isn’t obvious: finding a useful way to think about a system, testing it, and turning messy behavior into a clear mechanism. I’m increasingly interested in AI alignment and interpretability, and how a physicist’s toolkit can help. I value clear reasoning, intellectual honesty, and collaboration—and I’m always happy to talk across fields.

Preprint · Under review at PRR

Learning from almost nothing

We study neural networks trained on heavily corrupted inputs. In the high-noise, infinite-width limit, we derive an interpretable decision rule governed by class-centroid overlaps.

I initiated the project and developed its symmetry-based linear-response explanation within neural tangent kernel theory.

Published · Physical Review Research (2026)

Topological classification and adaptive state preparation

We connect the symmetry and topology of monitored free-fermion dynamics to adaptive protocols for preparing topological states.

I developed classification and existence proofs, an effective description of the dynamics, and numerical studies of the protocol’s topological phase transitions.

Critical boundaries in adaptive quantum circuits

I am investigating the critical and chiral properties of topological domain-wall modes prepared by measurement and feedback, connecting entanglement and charge-fluctuation diagnostics to the structure of boundary-state ensembles.
